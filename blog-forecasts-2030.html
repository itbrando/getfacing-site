What the 2030 forecasts for AI shopping actually count

Forecasts for AI agents in shopping run from a tenth of US online spending to $5 trillion worldwide. Most of the spread comes from what each one counts.

Facing AIOctober 7, 20265-minute read

Any case for preparing a store for AI shopping agents quotes a 2030 forecast, and the forecasts do not agree. Morgan Stanley’s range is 10–20% of US online spendingSource 1. Bain’s is 15–25%Source 2. McKinsey puts the ceiling at $1 trillion of US retail revenueSource 3, and Juniper Research expects $1.5 trillion of spending worldwideSource 4. Read closely, they disagree less than the headlines suggest, because each one measures something different.

The forecasts side by side

Why the numbers differ

Three choices explain most of the spread.

  1. Who counts as an agent. Bain counts retailers’ own assistants as well as independent ones such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot or Perplexity. The largest retailer assistant is Amazon’s: Amazon credits Rufus with “nearly $12 billion in incremental annualized sales” in 2025, by its own estimate and with no method givenSource 5. Sales like those happen inside one retailer’s store and never reach a brand’s own site.
  2. Whether influence counts. Bain counts purchases an agent started or influenced, not only those it completed. A shopper who asks an agent for options and then buys on the brand’s site is in Bain’s number.
  3. The base. Bain and Morgan Stanley size a share of US online sales, which ran at about $1.36 trillion a year in the second quarter of 2026: $340.2 billion for the quarter, seasonally adjusted, 17.1% of all retail salesSource 6. McKinsey sizes revenue across all US retail, online and in store, and adds a worldwide range. Juniper’s figure is worldwide only.

The two forecasts that measure nearly the same thing, Bain’s and Morgan Stanley’s, overlap at 15–20% of US online sales. That band is the most defensible single planning figure, and it is still a forecast.

How far today’s figures are from 2030

What stores can measure today is much smaller. Independent studies put visits from AI assistants at 0.1–0.3% of traffic to online stores between August 2024 and August 2026Sources 7,8,9. Growth is fast: Adobe measured AI-referred visits to US retail sites up 393% year over year in the first quarter of 2026 and 127% in AugustSource 10, and Shopify counted nearly 13 times as many AI-referred orders in the first quarter as a year earlierSource 11.

The two kinds of figure are not the same quantity. Visits that analytics can label as AI are a floor: of the assistants we observed, only ChatGPT tags its links as its own, and orders placed inside Copilot, Meta and Google do not load a store’s analytics tags or ad pixelsSources 12,14. Bain’s number also counts influence that never shows up as an AI visit. Even so, getting from under 1% of visits to a fifth of online sales in four years assumes that adoption keeps compounding. The forecasters say as much: Juniper expects trust to remain “the number one barrier” and agentic commerce to become “an important access channel” that will not replace store checkouts for the foreseeable futureSource 4. In Gartner’s surveys, 54% of shoppers who use AI double-check “all information”Source 13.

What every forecast assumes

Each forecast assumes that agents find the product, attach the right version and seller, and report a correct price and stock. When they do not, the sale does not disappear from the forecast; it moves to another product, another seller or nowhere. In our own research, at least 6.6% of the offers in 108 agents’ answers to shopping requests were wrong (42 of 636), nearly all a different product or the right product in the wrong size or pack, and in ChatGPT’s product panels the product’s own brand store held the default seller in 9 of 112Source 14. Those were test requests, not real shopping traffic, but they show where forecast dollars can leak away from a brand. We look at how price and stock go wrong in a separate note.

What a brand can take from the forecasts

  • Plan for the agent as a shopper, whichever forecast you prefer. Every one of them puts agent-driven sales at a meaningful share of online sales by 2030, from a small base today.
  • Expect much of the volume to run through someone else’s assistant. Retailers’ own assistants count in Bain’s number, and independent agents choose a seller for each product. A brand’s own store wins those sales only when agents can read its products correctly and choose it.
  • Do not size the channel from analytics alone. Untagged links and checkouts inside the agents mean a store’s reports undercount what agents already influence.
